Digestive System

Anatomy & Physiology • Chapter 7

Introduction

The digestive system is responsible for breaking down food into smaller molecules so that the body can absorb nutrients and eliminate waste. It is also called the gastrointestinal (GI) tract or alimentary canal.

Main Parts of the Digestive System

  • Mouth
  • Pharynx
  • Oesophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small Intestine (Duodenum, Jejunum, Ileum)
  • Large Intestine (Caecum, Colon, Rectum)
  • Anus
  • Accessory Organs: Liver, Gallbladder, Pancreas

Functions of Digestive System

  • Ingestion – Taking food into the mouth
  • Digestion – Breaking down complex food into simple forms
  • Absorption – Absorption of nutrients into blood
  • Assimilation – Using absorbed nutrients by body cells
  • Elimination / Egestion – Removal of undigested waste

Important Points for Exam

  • Length of small intestine ≈ 6–7 meters
  • Length of large intestine ≈ 1.5 meters
  • Stomach can hold 1.5–2 litres of food
  • Peristalsis helps in movement of food
  • Liver produces bile, Pancreas produces pancreatic juice
